Libraries and Public School Performance in Chicago

A group project investigating the correlation between school performance and proximity to public libraries in Chicago


This project was completed for GEO 425: Problems in Geographic Information Science at Michigan State University. I analyzed the relationship between public high school performance and proximity to public libraries in Chicago. Using data on the locations of libraries, public high schools, and road networks, I also incorporated school performance metrics such as four-year graduation rates and standardized test scores. In ArcGIS Pro, I refined the road network to include only drivable roads and calculated travel times using speed limit and road length attributes. I then performed a network analysis by designating each public library as a facility and mapping out 2- and 5-minute service areas around them. The resulting spatial data was converted to GeoJSON and visualized in kepler.gl, with high schools symbolized by graduation rates. Preliminary findings suggest that schools located within library service areas generally exhibit higher graduation rates. However, there are exceptions, and statistical analysis showed only a slight negative correlation between distance to libraries and school performance. Additional data such as funding levels for schools and libraries or socioeconomic demographics are needed along with further analysis to determine if the correlation is causal.
*Please note the map may take a few seconds to load and a popup may appear that can be closed


Hospital Distribution in Michigan

A research report investigating hospital distribution, financial stability, and health outcomes in Michigan Counties


This project was done as an honors opportunity for GEO 425: Problems in Geographic Information Science at Michigan State University. I utilized GIS to look into public health data of Michigan's counties to identify areas that were lacking in healthcare and had worse health outcomes. I investigated any correlations between access to hospitals and the life expectancy and number of hospital visits in each county. I also dived deeper into the financial status of each county by the percentage of people in poverty and the average household income. I used Hot Spot Analysis (Getis-Ord Gi*) in ArcGIS Pro to identify counties that had lower life expectancies or more citizens in poverty. My findings indicated wealthier counties had more hopsitals within them, but that population density played a bigger role in health outcomes than just proximity to medical facilities.

Grape Cluster Yield using LiDAR

A group project investigating if drones or handheld LiDAR systems are better at object identification


This project was completed collaboratively for GEO 829: Collection and Analysis of Drone Imagery at Michigan State University. My group and I investigated the research question are grape clusters better counted by Unoccupied Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) or by handheld Light Detection and Ranging (LiDAR) systems? We used CloudCompare to visually inspect the point clouds created using LiDAR which we then put through an unsupervised classification model to identify clusters of grapes. We used Pix4D to process the UAV data which was then also put through an unsupervised classification model. The UAV data also went through a deep learning model to identify objects/grape clusters. The results were preliminary and would require further investigation but LiDAR was better in this first stage of research.

Remote Sensing Approaches for Pre-Harvest Detection of Root Disease in Sugar Beets

A research project conducted with Dr. Erin Bunting, Dr. Rachel Naegele, and Dr. Linda Hanson, funded by the United States Department of Agriculture


This was a project funded by the United States Department of Agriculture and conducted under Michigan State University's Department of Geography, Environment, and Spatial Sciences and the Department of Plant, Soil and Microbial Sciences. The goal of the project was to utilize multispectral and hyperspectral imagery collected of sugar beet fields to identify healthy and diseased plots before harvest. In the past, few methodologies have been identified that can successfully pinpoint diseased plots with enough time to prevent root disease from infecting nearby plants. We were able to identify several vegetation indices that found significant differences in healthy and diseased plants early in the growing season. These indices included the Modified Photochemical Reflectance Index (MPRI) and the Green Leaf Index (GLI). A random forest model was utilized to confirm the indices were significant and at which dates imagery was collected were important in identifying differences. Field imagery was loaded, cleaned, and visualized in ArcGIS Pro following preprocessing procedures. Reflectance and band value extraction, random forest, and graphs were produced and analyzed in R. Our results are from the drone imagery collected in 2024. The next steps are to analyze the additional years of imagery we have collected to determine if spatiotemporal trends are consistent. I presented results from 2024 at the 2026 Michigan State University Undergraduate Research and Arts Forum (UURAF) and earned a first place award in the Agriculture and Animal Science category. At UURAF, I had the opportunity to share my results and the impact they could have on the research and sugar beet farming community.
